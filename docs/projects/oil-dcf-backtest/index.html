Weekly Oil-DCF Rebalancing Backtest

Every week, this strategy runs a fast Monte Carlo version of the Stochastic Macro-Driven DCF (v1.2) for each stock in a 12-name upstream oil universe, anchored to that Monday's WTI crude price. Stocks are ranked by how far their price sits below simulated fair value, and the portfolio is built long-only, tilted toward the cheapest names, with position sizes capped on a graduated schedule based on how many names look genuinely undervalued that week.

Methodology note — look-ahead bias

Company fundamentals (revenue, shares outstanding, net debt) are pulled directly from SEC EDGAR filing history and applied point-in-time — each week's valuation uses only what was actually public as of that date, not today's numbers projected backward. 11 of 12 tickers use this point-in-time data; BP falls back to a single static snapshot (its XBRL filing history lacks a usable shares-outstanding figure), so its contribution to the backtest still carries some look-ahead bias.

Reading these results

The strategy trails the benchmark on raw return, but with meaningfully lower volatility, a shallower max drawdown, and a better Sharpe ratio. That's a coherent pattern, not a contradiction: the DCF signal is giving up some upside capture in exchange for real downside protection, which is a defensible risk/return tradeoff on its own rather than a weaker version of the benchmark.

It's also worth naming directly: an earlier version of this backtest, run before the point-in-time fix above, showed the strategy beating the benchmark by roughly 16 points of alpha. Most of that apparent edge was an artifact of using 2026 fundamentals to make 2019 decisions. Alpha shrinking — or reversing — after removing a source of look-ahead bias is the expected, credible outcome, not a failure of the strategy.

Position sizing

Weights are set by relative undervaluation — (fair value − price) / fair value — normalized across all positively-signaled names, then capped on a schedule tied to how many names are eligible that week: 30% cap with 1 eligible name, rising to 50% with 2, 60% with 3, 65% with 4, 75% with 5, and 85% with 6, uncapped beyond that. When capped weights can't fully deploy capital (e.g. only one cheap name with a 30% cap), the remainder sits in cash rather than being forced into a less-convincing name.
